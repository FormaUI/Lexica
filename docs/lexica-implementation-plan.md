# Lexica Implementation Plan

This plan records the steps that take Lexica from what is built to what the [specification](lexica-specification.md) and the [design](lexica-design.md) describe, the decisions that shape them, the state of each step, and where the work departs from the design. The specification is authoritative for the language, and the design for its modules and tooling; this plan changes with the work and is retired when every step is done. [DOMQL's plan](https://github.com/FormaUI/DOMQL/blob/main/docs/domql-implementation-plan.md) records the DOM runtime's.

## Built

Each item is what exists and what checks it; the checks that run through DOMQL's entry point are DOMQL's, until Lexica evaluates on its own.

- **The language.** Parsing source text and creating expressions from their definition, with the position of every mistake, and writing a definition back as text, on one line or laid out as `domql.format` gives a request's canonical text. Checked by the tests of parsing, of the definition and of writing it.
- **Navigation and absence.** Access that requires a receiver that cannot be null, `?.`, optional enclosed accesses, the coalescing operator and bracket property access, each recorded in the definition and written back as canonical text, a watch following what each evaluates. Checked by the tests of parsing, of the definition, of resolution, of evaluation, of watches and of comparing snapshots.
- **Truthiness and Boolean expressions.** Null and false are falsey and every other value truthy; `and` and `or` answer the operand that decides them, typed by the parts of their operands that can decide them and refused where no one type holds those, `not` answers a Boolean, conditions and conditional bindings take their branch by truthiness, `where` takes a predicate whose truthiness its function receives, and stable references are narrowed on the paths a test decides, a watch following the operands and clauses evaluation reaches; tests are Boolean members, and `==` and `!=` compare strings, numbers, Booleans and nodes, refusing two types that fit neither way. Checked by the tests of parsing, of resolution, of evaluation, of writing, of watches and of modules.
- **Named indexers.** `receiver:name[key]` invokes an operation a module declares an indexer, with its one key, and `receiver:[key]` the receiver's default indexer, one for any receiver; `?:` reads a receiver that can be null, evaluating neither key nor indexer for null, and a bracket after an indexer is a bracket property access of what it answers. The built-in indexers are an element's `attribute`, its default, and `style`, its computed style, which replace `attributeOf` and `computedStyleOf`. Checked by the tests of parsing, of the definition and its canonical text, of modules and the registry, of resolving and evaluating indexers, built-in and declared by a module, and of watches.
- **Conversions.** `to` converts a value to another type of data through the one conversion the vocabulary declares from its type to that type, never a chain of them; a value already of the type answers itself, null answers null without converting, and a value the conversion rejects fails the evaluation with an evaluation error; `try … to "type"` carries the same conversion out and answers null for a rejection, and a conversion that throws, or answers a value not of its type, fails the evaluation under either form. `if let name = value` takes its consequent where its value is not null, with the name bound to it there alone. The built-in conversions take a string to a number by HTML's valid floating-point syntax after trimming and to a Boolean from `"true"` and `"false"` alone, and a number and a Boolean to a string, and a module declares conversions to and from its own types. Checked by the tests of parsing, of the definition and its canonical text, of modules and the registry, of resolving and evaluating conversions and their tests, and of watches, in a simulated DOM, and by the README's example.
- **Bindings and resolution.** Every binding typed from its value, and a definition resolved against the vocabulary and its result typed, without a browser; the resolution is kept for its definition, so the queries made from one text share it. Checked by the resolver tests and the tests of `query.resolve`.
- **Conditions, and members as calls and indexers.** Modules declare conditions, Booleans of their receivers each under a contract of its own, and each is asked by its one verb, `.is` for a state, `.has` for a possession or a presence and `.supports` for a capability, an ordinary call whose name the expression's resolution settles; the built-in conditions have native semantics, and an element's ARIA view answers its `checked` and `disabled` conditions from their attributes and reads any `aria-` attribute as written through its default indexer, observed through an observation argument with a prefix. Every member is accessed, called or indexed: `matches`, `intersects`, `overlaps` and `on` are calls, `:media[…]` is the window's indexer of a media query and `item` a list's default indexer, and the infix form is gone from the grammar, the definition, the canonical text and module declarations, each place refusing it with the form to write. Checked by the tests of conditions, of parsing, of resolution and of the documents' examples, and in Chromium.
- **The syntax tree.** The parser reads a text into D14's lossless syntax tree, a node for each construct named after its grammar rule and every token with the trivia before it, which rebuilds the text exactly, comments included; the definition and its locations are lowered from the tree, which a kept query does not retain, keeping its text instead. The tokenizer also reads any text tolerantly, token types and node kinds are listed for tooling, internal until its API, and every syntax error passes through one place in the parser, the foundation for recovery D14 sets out. Measured against the parser before it, on the documents' queries, a 60-field query and malformed texts: the documents' queries 7% slower to parse and malformed texts 8% faster, the large query within 3%, its transient allocations two-thirds higher, what a kept query retains unchanged, and the bundle 3% larger. Checked by the tests of the tree and the tokenizer, and by every existing test unchanged.
- **Arithmetic.** `+`, `-`, `*`, `/` and `%` on numbers, and `-` negating one, at the ordinary precedences, left to right, tighter than an equality and looser than a conversion; an operand that can be null, or is no number, is a validation error, a division or a remainder by zero and a result no finite number holds are evaluation errors at the operation, `%` keeps the sign of the dividend, and `??` stands apart from binary arithmetic unless a group holds one of them. `-` is always an operator, a negation of a numeric literal being that negative literal, so `a-b` subtracts. Arithmetic reads what its operands read, left then right, and a computed number is compared exactly. Checked by the tests of parsing, resolution, evaluation, formatting, watching and comparing.
- **Comparisons.** `<`, `<=`, `>` and `>=` on numbers, answering a Boolean, and a chain of them pointing one way, strict and inclusive mixed, recorded as one comparison of its operands and operators; a chain that turns is a syntax error at the operator that turns it, a comparison stands apart from an equality and from `??` unless a group holds one of them, and an operand that can be null, or is no number, is a validation error. Operands are evaluated left to right, each at most once, the first comparison that is false answering false without evaluating the rest, so a watch follows only what was evaluated. Checked by the tests of parsing, of the definition, resolution, evaluation, formatting and watching.
- **String escapes.** A string escapes a double quote, a backslash, a line feed, a carriage return, a tab and the escape character as `\"`, `\\`, `\n`, `\r`, `\t` and `\e`, and any Unicode scalar value as `\u{…}`, one to six hexadecimal digits; a surrogate, a value above 10FFFF and any other escape are syntax errors at their backslash, and every character may be written as itself too. The canonical text escapes the line breaks, the tab and the escape character by their letters, and every other control, format or separator character but the space as `\u{…}`, so no invisible character stays hidden. Checked by the tests of parsing and formatting.
- **Interpolated strings.** `$"…"` joins its text with the values its interpolations insert, each `{`, one expression and `}`, its text a string's with each brace doubled and an escape read whole; an interpolation inserts a string as it is and a number as the built-in conversion writes it, and a value that can be null, or is no string or number, is a validation error. The interpolations are evaluated once each, left to right, and the text compared exactly. Recorded as one node of its text and its expressions in order, the tokenizer reading the string's text and its interpolations' expressions in modes of their own, so the syntax tree stays lossless, and written with each interpolation on one line, a space after its `{` where an object expression begins it. Checked by the tests of the tokenizer, the tree, parsing, the definition, resolution, evaluation, formatting and watching.
- **External bindings and keyword names.** `~name` reads the caller's binding of a name wherever it stands, in scope of a local of its name, before one, or named by a reserved word, and a definition's parameter is always the caller's binding, the scope rules of a name written alone staying the text's; the canonical text writes `~` wherever a name alone would read something else. After `.`, `?.`, `:` and `?:` a keyword names a member or an indexer, a literal's word naming none, and a module may declare one so named. Checked by the tests of parsing, of the definition, resolution, formatting, registration and result paths.
- **Bare names and values by `=`.** Every name is written bare and reads the local its lexical scope declares, and otherwise the caller's binding, a name before a later local of its own refused; `@` is a syntax error that gives the bare name, and a field, a local and a binding take their value after `=`, a colon there refused with the form to write. Checked by the tests of parsing, of the definition and of resolution, every query of the documents and the tests rewritten and keeping its meaning.
- **Bodies.** The root expression, a `do … end` block and every conditional branch are bodies: an expression alone, or locals, each `let`, a name, `=` and a value, and the `return` that gives the result and completes the body alone, which nothing follows, the conditional closed by `end` and its branches all giving their results with `return` or none of them. Locals are evaluated once each, in order, whenever the body runs, an unused one included, each in scope after its value and none before it, a nested body hiding an enclosing name; a `do` block and a conditional are grouped as operands and never a chain's receiver, and a `do` block settles a fixed argument only where every local and its result do. Recorded as a `body` node of its locals and its result, and written with each local and the result on a line, a branch body a level within its clause. Checked by the tests of parsing, of the definition, resolution, evaluation, formatting and watching, and every conditional of the documents and the tests, given its `end`, keeps its meaning.
- **Functions.** `function(name, …) body end` is a local's value, called by the local's name with as many positional arguments as it declares, each evaluated once, left to right, in the caller's scope; its body follows a body's rules and sees the locals and the implicit parameter where it is declared, never the caller's, which a call captures anew at each evaluation. Each call resolves a copy of the body of its own against its arguments' types, an error in it located at the function's text, and a function nothing calls is never resolved beyond its declaration. No function calls itself or one declared after it, and none is passed, held, compared or returned; a call settles no fixed argument. Checked by the tests of parsing, of the definition, resolution, evaluation, formatting and watching, and in Chromium, a watch following what a call reads, the branch its body selects and what it captures.
- **Simple expressions and positional arguments.** An argument of a call, a member's or a local function's, an indexer's key, a bracket property access's name and an interpolation are simple expressions: no `do` block, local, conditional binding, `return` or function, and no conditional beyond an `if` and an `else` branch of simple values, which holds no other, through everything written within them, parentheses included, validation enforcing it on text and definitions alike. A function stands as a local's initializer alone, and a body's locals and an object expression's are one `local` production. A member call takes any number of arguments by position, its signature deciding how many: a positional input supplies its parameters in order, its required ones first, and an options input one object; a call's definition holds `arguments`, in version 25. Checked by the tests of parsing, of the definition, of resolution and of a module's registration, the examples the grammar's brief gives among them.
- **Reserved names, field scope and roots.** `to` is a keyword beside `as`, and every declaration, a field, a member, a named indexer, a local, a function, a parameter and a conditional binding, takes any word, a reserved one included, written as it is, while a bare reserved word keeps its meaning where an expression stands. `@` before a reserved word reads the nearest local or field of that name, a structure error where none is in scope, and `~` reads the caller's binding of any name. A field's name is in scope after its value, through the fields after it, its value reused; a local, a field and a conditional binding hide an outer name, two of one scope never sharing one. `document` and `window` are `root` nodes, so a `parameter` is always a caller's binding, in version 26, and the canonical text writes `@` and `~` where a name needs them. Checked by the tests of text, of definitions, of resolution, of watching and of canonical round trips, every reserved word in every position among them.
- **The implicit parameter.** `context`, or `~` alone, refers to the value against which the current expression is evaluated, a `context` node in version 27, written `context` by the canonical text, while `~` before a name still reads the caller's binding; `self` is an ordinary name. Checked by the tests of text, of definitions and of canonical text, `context` and `~` side by side among them.
- **Aligned with the specification.** The reorganized specification was reviewed against the implementation, and each disagreement settled. A projection of a list whose items can be null is a list of objects that can be null; a `do` block is a `do` node of its own, so a conditional's branch body and a block written as its expression stay apart; a conditional whose branches use `return` takes `else if … end` whose branches do; a cast's whole target is validated, so every accepted definition writes text that parses, as a conditional binding's value reading its own name's binding with `~` does; `text-editable` follows the native `contenteditable` rules; `window.size` follows a viewport scrollbar appearing or going through its visual viewport's `resize`. In definition version 28 a body's locals are `locals`; a module declares an event source's kind as `eventSource` and an event type's `data`; the tokenizer's tokens are `caller-binding`, `interpolation-start` and `interpolation-end`. Internal names, messages and test descriptions follow the specification's terms, and the specification says what the implementation does, its grammar the intended language, its examples teaching what each meant to and its Terms complete. Checked by the tests of each fix, the examples of the documents with their results, and in Chromium with its scrollbars shown, a watch of `window.size` following each scrollbar.
- **The object root.** `root` joins the roots, `{ kind: "root", name: "root" }` in version 29, read as `@root` for a local or a field and `~root` for a caller's binding; written inside an object expression, it refers to the outermost object being built, which nested objects and projections share, a projection's receiver outside it, and each evaluation, event and item of an outermost projection builds anew. A field is read through it as `root.bar` or `root[name]`, only one whose initializer has completed where `root` is written, a reference outside every object expression a structure error. Preparation types it by its outermost object and those fields, kept through locals, arguments, results and conditionals, a function keeping the fields where it is declared and a parameter taking the argument's; two merge only with one object and the same fields, and no field or result holds one. An options object follows the same rules, its options evaluated once each in the order written, each in scope by its name and available through root after its initializer as the value and type given, a default neither, and an expression or a predicate option, which the member evaluates with the names and root where it is written, refused where read as a value. A field read through it keeps the branch it selected and its member's tolerance, and a watch compares a value a `do` block or a local function's call gives as the expression its body gives. Checked by the tests of each rule and refusal, of round trips, and of a watch reusing a branch and a tolerance through an alias, an argument and a returned reference.
- **Function values and arrow functions.** An arrow function, `() => 42`, `row => row.visible` or `(left, right) => left - right`, parentheses optional for one parameter, holds one simple expression as its body, its result; it is a local's initializer or an argument, `argument = simple-expression | arrow-function`, and a regular function written directly as an argument is refused, as is an arrow whose body is no simple expression, `x => y => x + y` among them. Either form is a closure keeping the scope, `context` and object root of its declaration, and a value within the expression: a local holds and aliases it, a call passes it, a conditional binding, a conditional, `??` or `or` chooses between functions, a function returns one, and a call through a local or a parameter is checked against each function it may be, their results merged and the one invoked recorded so a watch follows it; no field or result holds one, and none is compared, interpolated, converted or given to a value parameter. A call reaching a function within its own call, as `invoke(invoke)` does through a parameter, is a validation error. A module's operation declares a function parameter, its callback contract the argument types and the result type a function given to it is checked against in preparation and at each call, callable only while the member's invocation lasts and given to no observation; the built-in `where`, `max`, `min` and `sum` keep their contextual expressions, an arrow given to one refused as such. A definition marks the arrow form `arrow: true`, in version 30, validated as text is. Checked by the tests of each form and refusal, of passing, returning and choosing functions, of closures, of recursion, of callback contracts and their lifetime, of round trips, and of watches following a chosen function and a callback's reads.
- **Raw strings and blocks.** A string, `"…"`, escapes with backslashes and a raw string, `'…'`, keeps them as text, doubling its quote to hold one; `$` interpolates either, `{{` and `}}` its literal braces, and without `$` every brace is text. Each is inline, on one line, a line break written in it refused, or a block: three or more quotes and a line break open it, as many close it first after the indentation of a later line, where the source continues, and that indentation is the margin removed from each line before escapes and interpolation, LF, CRLF and a lone CR read as LF and a run of quotes as long as the delimiter in its text refused. An interpolation stands on one line, and one beginning with an object expression groups it, `{({ x = 1 }.x)}`. Every form that does not interpolate stands where a string literal is required. Tokens keep each string's quote and delimiter length for formatting that keeps the written form, which editor tooling builds; canonical text writes `"…"` and `$"…"`, and definitions, version 30, record values and parts as before. Checked by the tests of each form, refusal, margin, line ending and quote run, of locations within blocks and of round trips.

What is not built: Lexica's own evaluation, apart from DOMQL, and editor tooling.

## Decisions

Each decision is open until its state says otherwise, and a step that needs it does not start before. A decision that is built leaves the plan once the specification or the design states what it decided; one a remaining step builds on stays, with its state.

### Settled

Each decision here is answered without building it, with what was found and what would reopen it.

- **Saying which declaration a value means.** Not adopted beyond a cast: only an extension's declarations can make a request ambiguous, since the built-in vocabulary makes none. A cast to a kind of node says which declaration a null receiver means, so `(thing as "element")?.ping()` takes the element's for a null `thing`, as a test shows. Nothing says it for a null receiver whose declarations are on types that are no nodes, which a cast cannot name, or for an empty list more than one declaration takes, as one on `list<number>` and one on `list<string>` both take a `list<never>`; each is refused as ambiguous.
  - **Reopened by** an extension whose declarations of one name collide in this way in real use. A declared parameter type is the leading candidate, and that use would show whether it covers the case.

### Open

The open decisions are taken a group at a time, in this order, each group one kind of work, until every one is answered: built, or settled with why it is not and what would reopen it.

#### 1. The language

What an expression can say that it cannot yet.

- **A flat-tree parent.** A parent across slots, beside the tree's own `parent` and `root`, waits until its uses and its meaning are clear.
- **The spelling of annotations.** `name: type = value` is free for an annotation once fields, locals and bindings take `=`; whether an annotation writes its type bare, as `count: number`, while a cast and a conversion quote theirs, as `as "element"` and `to "number"` do, or every one of them writes it the same way, is decided when annotations are designed.

#### 2. Collections

What a collection is, designed as a task of its own on the conversions built.

- **A collection model.** DOMQL has one collection, `list<T>`, an array, where hosts hand over arrays, DOM collections, sets, maps and plain objects, and the C# half will hand over its own. One native model and representation is designed, with host collections adapted into it, before any operation that depends on what a collection is: ranges of a list, as `slice`, `take` and `drop`, and conversions to and from collections, as to `"array"`, `"object"` or `"entries"`, wait on it. Until then a conversion from or to a list is refused at registration, a deferral and no consequence of what a conversion is, since a conversion of a list can be as pure as any other; a structured type with a list among its fields converts as any other structured type of data does.

### D14. A syntax tree for tooling

Accepted; its tree, the lowering from it and the foundation tooling needs are built, ahead of the grammar bare names, bodies and functions added, with the parser strict and every part internal; recovery, a public tooling API and an editor's integration are built by step 14. One error-tolerant parser builds a lossless syntax tree of the text, its tokens and their trivia, comments among them, and the definition is lowered from that tree, so there is one grammar and one parser, and the definition stays the only contract the runtime reads and evaluates. The tree is internal until step 14's tooling API exposes it.

- **Nodes for constructs.** A node stands for a construct written, named after the grammar rule that produces it, as `equality`, `conjunction` and `dot-property-access` are; a rule that only passes an operand on, a level of precedence or a repetition, produces no node of its own, so `panel` holds no wrapper for each level it passes through. Tokens and trivia carry the complete text, so the tree gives the text back character for character.
- **Recovery for the editor alone.** The parser records a missing token and skips an unexpected one, so a text being written gives a tree and multiple useful diagnostics without excessive cascades. Creating a query from text still refuses any syntax error; a recovered tree is read by tooling, and a missing token is never taken as permission to guess what the text meant.
- **Tooling, apart from the runtime.** Token types and node kinds are listed for tooling, contracts within DOMQL until step 14 stabilizes a public tooling API, and the tokenizer reads any text tolerantly, an invalid token holding its diagnostic standing for each text that is no token, so a highlighter or an editor reads tokens, trivia, ranges and structure without making or preparing a query. Every syntax error the parser reports passes through one place, which a strict parse throws from: a foundation for recovery, which also needs points to resume from, missing and unexpected tokens handled and progress through the text guaranteed, and which is built into this parser, so an editor's parser is this one, never a copy of the grammar and never this one with its reporting overridden. Syntax highlighting reads tokens and nodes directly, and semantic highlighting adds what resolution knows of each name.
- **A query's tree, for a later `query.toSyntaxTree()`.** A query made from text keeps that text beside its definition, through its locations, and its tree is read from it again where a tool asks, so it is the original source, comments included. A query made from a definition has no source; its tree is the one read from the canonical text `domql.format` writes for it, marked generated, its ranges that text's. Neither a prepared query nor a watch keeps a tree for tooling.
- **From syntax to meaning.** Lowering records the syntax node each definition node comes from, and a resolution maps each definition node to its declarations and types, so tooling joins a position in the text to what resolution knows of it: completion after `panel.` reads the receiver's type, and renaming a local follows the scope resolution records for it and refuses a name another binding would capture.
- **Formatting.** A query keeps no syntax tree, so the runtime's memory is as it is, and `domql.format` keeps its meaning, the canonical text of a request, which no comment survives. Formatting that preserves comments is a tooling operation over a document's syntax tree.
- **Measured before it is committed:** uncached parsing and lowering, their allocations, the memory a tree retains and the bundle's size, against the current parser, on valid input and on malformed input, an editor's usual text; a watch's refresh, which never touches a syntax tree, stays as it is.
- **Deferred until a need is concrete:** incremental reparsing, specialized tree machinery, and a public tree schema shared by C# and JavaScript.

## Steps

A step is done when its tests pass in the gate, its documents say what it built, and its state here says so. Completed steps leave the plan. Built describes the capabilities they delivered.

The remaining work is separating the language from the DOM, then its packages, then the beta, then editor tooling.

### 31. Separate the language from the DOM

Lexica, an extensible expression language that knows no domain, holds the grammar, definitions, types, resolution, evaluation and vocabulary contracts; DOMQL keeps the DOM vocabulary, observation, watching, events, actions, behaviors and its query API, and uses Lexica as its language. Each change is made here and in DOMQL together, DOMQL consuming this repository through a local link until a package is released.

- **State:** in progress. The language has its own repository, whose history begins with its port from DOMQL; its specification, design and plan are its own, and a test refuses an import from outside `src/`. Its resolver, writer and syntax-tree tests that run through DOMQL's entry point stay DOMQL's, as integration tests, until Lexica evaluates on its own. The names are Lexica's, its built-in vocabulary is the domain-independent one and the DOM vocabulary is DOMQL's `domql` module, its entry point `lexica` reads, checks and writes definitions, creates modules and resolves against registries of its own, and `domql.query`, `domql.format`, `domql.createModule` and `domql.info` are built on it. No module is built in: DOMQL's `domql` is made with `createModule` as any module is, a module declares members and conditions of any name on any type, and Lexica's own module, which Lexica creates itself, is the one exempt from the conversion ownership rule; colliding declarations are still refused at registration until owner precedence lands. Lexica's `Evaluator` evaluates a resolved request for its host, with `beforeInvoke`, `afterInvoke` and `afterEvaluation`, and DOMQL's `QueryEvaluator` is the observing host over it, holding the observations its hooks report; implementations keep the positional contract until the module declarations of version 32. Lexica speaks of source and expressions, never queries: version 31 names the definition's root `expression`, its pointers starting `/expression`, a value-producing request's kind `value`, the syntax tree's root `source` and the tokenizer's `~name` token `caller-binding`, while DOMQL keeps the word for its own query API.
- **Namespaces.** Each module exposes its vocabulary through its namespace, named as the module is: DOMQL registers the module `domql`, and an expression reads `domql.document`, `domql.window` and the module's other properties and functions through that namespace. These are **module members**: they belong to the module, which declares them on itself rather than on a type, and the namespace is only the name text reaches them by.
  - **Module members.** `document` and `window` are module members of `domql`, properties, each declaring its type, how it changes and its implementation by the member contracts, as every member does. Reading one through a namespace never implies its value is fixed across evaluations.
  - **Names.** A module's name follows Lexica's rules for names and is unique in a registry, so a second module of one name fails registration. Members within a module follow the member declaration and collision rules, and different modules may expose module members of one name, as `domql.document` and `office.document` do. Names compare as every Lexica name does, and every module follows the same rules, Lexica's own aside, which has no namespace.
  - **Extensions.** A module may declare members on any type, its own or another module's: owning a type says who declares it, and the rule naming a member outside a module's own types after the module is already removed. An unqualified member resolves by the type's owner: an applicable member the receiver type's owning module declares wins over every extension; otherwise one applicable extension resolves as any member does; and several applicable extensions are refused as ambiguous when the expression is resolved, before anything is evaluated. `domql` owns `element` and declares `size`, so once `charts` declares a `size` extension too, both register, `panel.size` reads `domql`'s and `charts.size(panel)` reads `charts`'; Lexica owns `list`, so `rows.count` reads Lexica's whatever extensions declare. Where the owner has no applicable member, competing extensions still register, since refusing one would leave its qualified form unusable: when `statistics` and `analytics` both declare `median` on lists, `values.median` is ambiguous, and the error names the member, its receiver's type, every competing module and the qualified forms that resolve it, as "Member `median` on `list<number>` is ambiguous between modules `statistics` and `analytics`. Use `statistics.median(values)` or `analytics.median(values)` to select one." Applicability follows the receiver and the access form, so one name on unrelated types is never ambiguous. The rule follows ownership alone and applies to every module alike.
  - **Qualified access.** Every member a module declares on a type is also reached through the module's namespace, the receiver its first argument and the member's own arguments after it, as `statistics.median(values)` and `charts.size(panel)`. The qualified form selects that module's declaration and reuses its implementation and evaluation contract, so no module declares a second implementation to make its extension addressable, and the ordinary form stays available beside it.
  - **A module's namespace.** A module's members, the qualified forms of the members and the predicates it declares on types, take part in its collision checks, so a module whose own declarations would make `statistics.median(values)` ambiguous is refused when it is validated.
  - **Indexers.** The indexer forms stand as they are: `panel.closest(".row")` calls a function, `panel:attribute["aria-label"]` reads the named indexer, `panel:["aria-label"]` the default one, and `?:attribute[…]` and `?:[…]` their optional forms. A default indexer is a named indexer designated as the default, so `:["aria-label"]` on an element selects `:attribute["aria-label"]` and `:[2]` on a list selects `:item[2]`, never a member without a name. Indexers follow the owner's precedence, so ordinary access to `domql`'s `attribute` indexer stays unambiguous whatever extension competes with it, and a default access selects the designated declaration. Selecting an extension's indexer explicitly keeps its key, its receiver and its evaluation contract; how a qualified indexer is spelled is settled separately.
  - **Predicates.** A predicate is asked with its declared verb, as `panel.is("hover")` and `panel.has("focus")`, and follows the owner's precedence: an applicable predicate the receiver type's owner declares wins, otherwise one applicable extension predicate resolves, and several are refused as ambiguous when the expression is resolved. `domql` owns `element` and declares `hover`, so `panel.is("hover")` reads `domql`'s whatever extension competes. A qualified predicate is called directly through its module, the receiver first and no verb, as `domql.hover(panel)` and `charts.hover(panel)`, selecting that predicate and reusing its implementation and evaluation contract; the verb belongs to ordinary access alone, and a qualified predicate's name takes part in its module's collision checks, its module members among them.
  - **Lexica's own module.** Lexica owns the language's types, `list`, `number`, `string` and `boolean` among them, and declares its members on them, as `rows.count` and `rows.where(it.visible)` read; that ownership is internal, so `lexica` is no namespace and no reserved word in text. Owner precedence keeps Lexica's members unambiguous, so no entry point is needed to tell them apart. Should Lexica later expose functions or properties of the language itself, its entry point is spelled outside what a module's name may be, as `~.member(…)` could be now that bare `~` no longer reads the implicit parameter; the spelling stays open, and none is introduced now.
  - **Registration.** A registry checks a module whole before it registers anything, so a module refused leaves none of its declarations registered.
  - **Text.** A module's name is reserved in text read through its registry, and its members' names are not: `domql.document` reads the module's property, `@domql` the nearest local or field named `domql`, and `~domql` the caller's binding. A module's name never collides with a word the language reserves. `let window = domql.window` declares an ordinary local, by the scope rules that already stand.
  - **Language names.** `context` is renamed `it`, the implicit parameter: the value an object expression follows, a projection's current item and the item an expression or predicate argument is evaluated for, as `rows.where(it.visible)` reads each row's `visible`. Members of a type are read through it as through any value of the type. `it` is reserved and `context` no longer is, and bare `~`, a second spelling of the implicit parameter, is removed. The specification, the design, comments and messages call it the implicit parameter, the name "evaluation context" leaving with `context`. `root` stays the object root, and `~name` stays the caller's binding.
  - **Reading and writing.** A module's name decides how a text reads, so a registry reads and writes text with its own module names, and `lexica.parse` and `lexica.format` know the language's alone. Nothing reads such a text otherwise unannounced: a registry refuses a definition read from text without its module names that names one of them, saying to read the text with the registry; a namespace naming no registered module is refused as one the registry lacks; and `@name` on a word the language does not reserve says that a module's name is read with its registry. Parsed texts are kept by their text and the module names they were read with, and registering a module whose name an earlier definition uses refuses that definition as one read without the registry's module names, never reading it again silently.
- **Module declarations.** Version 32 gives a module's declarations one shape, every one naming what it applies to and carrying its own code:
  - **The module.** A module is `{ name, types, members, predicates, eventTypes, observationTypes, conversions }`; its `name` is the module's, and so its namespace's.
  - **Targets.** A member or a predicate names its `target`, replacing `on`: a type, a list of types, or `any`. A member without a target is a module member, read through the namespace, as `domql.window`, and its implementation is given no receiver; a predicate always names one. A target the module's own `types` declare is the module's own; a target another module declares is an extension of it, and `any` and the language's types are Lexica's. A target no module declares is refused when the module is registered, so a misspelled type never becomes an extension.
  - **Members.** `kind` is `property` or `function`, `function` replacing `operation`, or `eventSource`, `action` or `behavior`. `syntax`, replacing `form`, is `function-call` or `indexer`, since every member written with arguments in parentheses, whatever its kind, is a function call; the specification's *Call* becomes *Function call*, written `receiver.function(…)`. `arguments`, replacing `input`, is `positional` or `options`, and `result` stays the type a member gives back.
  - **Updates.** A member's or a predicate's change contract is grouped under `updates`: `changes`, `misses`, `reads`, `tolerance` and `observations`. A member that reads no value, an action or a behavior, leaves it out.
  - **Predicates.** A module's predicates, replacing its conditions, are the yes-or-no states asked by name with a verb, as `panel.is("hover")`; each names its `target`, its `verb`, its `updates` and its `implementation`. They are predicates as the arguments an expression writes for `where` and `first` are, the language's own; the parameter kind keeps the name `predicate`.
  - **Implementations.** Each declaration that runs code carries it as `implementation`: members, predicates, observation types and conversions. The module's separate functions object and each declaration's `function` key are removed, so a member's name is never written twice; two members sharing code refer to one JavaScript function. `implementation` is optional, and a module that leaves it out declares a vocabulary that is resolved against and never evaluated, as DOMQL's declarations alone are in its tests. Every key but `implementation` is data, so tooling reads a declaration without running it.
  - **What an implementation receives.** An implementation is called with one object and takes what it needs from it by name, never by position, so none accepts what it ignores to reach what it reads and a field added later moves nothing. A member's or a predicate's holds `receiver`, the value it is read from, which a module member has none of; `args`, the expression's arguments by parameter name, `args` since strict mode refuses `arguments` as a name; `env`, the environment, what the host supplies for the evaluation, named as JavaScript hosts commonly name it; and, for an event source, `deliver`, which each event is handed to, and for a member reading maintained values, `samples`, its observations' latest. An observation type's holds `request`, `notify` and `env`, and a conversion's `value` and `reject`. The contract is JavaScript's alone, and no expression is written differently for it.
  - **The helpers.** The declaration helpers follow the shape, `function` and `predicate` replacing `operation` and `condition`.
  - **The example.** DOMQL's `closest`, a member of `domql`'s own `element`:
    ```js
    {
        name: 'closest',
        target: 'element',
        kind: 'function',
        syntax: 'function-call',
        arguments: 'positional',
        result: 'element?',
        parameters: [{ name: 'selector', kind: 'value', type: 'string', required: true, fixed: false, nulls: 'propagate' }],
        updates: { changes: 'partly-observable', misses: 'pointer state such as :hover and control state such as :checked', reads: 'fresh', observations: [ … ] },
        implementation: ({ receiver, args }) => receiver.closest(args.selector),
    }
    ```
    `domql.window` is a member without a target, `{ name: 'window', kind: 'property', result: 'window', implementation: ({ env }) => env.window }`, and `hover` a predicate, `{ name: 'hover', target: 'element', verb: 'is', updates: { changes: 'partly-observable', misses: '…', reads: 'fresh', observations: [ … ] }, implementation: ({ receiver }) => receiver.matches(':hover') }`.
- **Reference types.** A module declares a reference type by its name, an optional single supertype, a required test and whether it is abstract. The test decides whether a value belongs to the type; abstractness only forbids automatic classification from answering the type as its final result. Classification runs the tests of the types with no supertype and descends into the children of each type whose test passed, answering the deepest type that is not abstract; two siblings or two unrelated types that both pass make it ambiguous, and a value whose deepest passing type is abstract is unclassified, both refusing the binding with a structure error naming the types. A host may type a binding explicitly: the value must pass the tests of the declared type and every supertype, without inference choosing among unrelated types, and the declared type is the binding's static type, so a more specific runtime type exposes no further member; `as` narrows it, checked by the tests. `==` compares two references of one hierarchy by identity, no result holds one, and DOMQL declares `node`, abstract, with `element`, `document`, `documentFragment` and `shadowRoot` beneath it, replacing the node kinds the type system hardcodes. Collection-entry matching stays separate: the evaluator records the source reference of each item a projection of a reference type gives, and the comparer matches entries by reference only where every reference is unique in both lists, by position otherwise.
- **The evaluation hooks.** An evaluation is given a host, whose state belongs to that evaluation unless the host chooses to share it, as a watch shares its sessions across evaluations. The host supplies the evaluation's environment, which implementations receive as their `env`, as DOMQL's `window` and `document` properties read theirs from it, and three hooks: `beforeInvoke(request)`, called before a member's implementation is invoked, which answers its samples or that one is pending, the implementation then not invoked at all, `afterInvoke(request, value)`, called only after a member's implementation answered a value that conforms, and `afterEvaluation(outcome)`, called once in a `finally` with the evaluation completed, pending or failed, whichever it is. `beforeInvoke` reports every read an evaluation attempts, whether it then succeeds, fails or is pending, so an observation covers a read whose implementation threw; `afterInvoke` reports only those that answered a value. An evaluation runs to one of its three outcomes synchronously, so none is cancelled while it runs: a watch cancels between evaluations, and `afterEvaluation` has no fourth outcome. Reading a module member is an ordinary member read, through `beforeInvoke` and `afterInvoke`, with the observation and the errors of any other member. A host with nothing to observe supplies the environment alone. Only the invocations an evaluation reaches call `beforeInvoke`, so short-circuited operands and unselected branches record nothing, and callbacks keep their lifetimes, closed before `afterEvaluation`. Each evaluation keeps its own state, so an evaluation an implementation starts is isolated from the one that called it. A throwing `beforeInvoke` or `afterInvoke` fails the evaluation at that member; a throwing `afterEvaluation` after a failure leaves the original failure thrown, the cleanup failure recorded on it, and after a success fails the evaluation itself. Without a host, an evaluation reads fresh members alone. Waiting for samples, cancellation between evaluations and watch scheduling stay in DOMQL.
- **Registries.** Lexica gives each host a registry of its own, with no shared registry; DOMQL's `domql.registerModule` keeps a default registry holding the DOM vocabulary.
- **The host API.** DOMQL imports Lexica's entry point alone, and nothing under `language/` or `common/`. The API is what DOMQL still reads from them once evaluation has moved here, so most of what its evaluator reads today, a resolution's per-node resolutions, a registry's implementations and types, and type parsing, never becomes API. The needs below are expected rather than a contract: DOMQL's use once evaluation has moved decides each one.
  - **Modules.** A host creates its modules with `createModule`, as DOMQL's `domql` is, and declares their members with the declaration helpers the entry point offers; no factory makes a module built in.
  - **Registries.** A registry answers its revision, which a host's resolution cache checks, and the members, the observation types and the module owning a declaration, which observation and evaluation look up.
  - **Resolutions.** `resolve` takes plain bindings and answers the whole resolution: its kind and type, the declarations it uses and what each node resolved to, the key a host caches it by, where a pointer stands in its text, and the type and the value a result path reaches.
  - **Watch policy.** Whether a request may be watched, and whether partial observation is accepted, are DOMQL's to decide from the change categories of the declarations a resolution uses; the resolver takes neither option.
  - **Utilities.** DOMQL keeps its own copy of the object and array operations its snapshots and evaluator use.
  - **Order.** The evaluator's core and its hooks move first, with the host API they leave DOMQL needing; then DOMQL consumes the package's API alone; then namespaces and reference types.
- **Names.** `DomqlError` becomes `LexicaError`, `DomqlModule` and `ModuleRegistry` become `LexicaModule` and `LexicaRegistry`, the definition's declared types become `Lexica…`, and the evaluator's core becomes Lexica's `Evaluator`, DOMQL's observing host taking the rest of `QueryEvaluator`. `domql.query`, `DomqlQuery`, `watch` and `subscribe` keep their names, and `domql.format` and `domql.createModule` delegate to Lexica's.
- **Definitions.** Version 31, built: the definition's `query` becomes `expression`. Version 32: a root node names the language's object root alone, and a namespace node names a module, a property or a call through it taking the member forms that already stand, its structure checked by its names and its meaning by resolution against a registry holding the module; the context node becomes the `it` node. The node kinds moving into DOMQL's vocabulary keep DOMQL's meaning.
- **Shown by** the existing DOMQL tests passing through the hooks unchanged, watching, waiting, partial observation, tolerance, disposal and callbacks among them; a test of a plain application-data host that registers a vocabulary, resolves types and evaluates through Lexica's entry points with a pending member and each outcome of `afterEvaluation`; tests of namespaces: module members read through their module's namespace, owner precedence for members, indexers and predicates, an extension selected by its qualified form, competing extensions registering and refused as ambiguous only where an expression uses one unqualified, with the diagnostic naming each module and its qualified form, a qualified predicate called directly and read with its own evaluation contract, a module refused for a collision within its own namespace, two modules of one name, members of one name in two modules, a module's name reserved in text, a registry reading, writing and resolving text with its module names, and qualified indexers once their spelling is settled; of classification, ambiguity, abstract types and explicit binding types, and of a hook that throws; and the boundary test.
- **The boundary still to draw.** The evaluator's core moves here from DOMQL's `QueryEvaluator`. The DOM vocabulary's members and observation targets that concern a node's root, as `root` on a node and an observation of the receiver's root or roots, keep their meaning in DOMQL's module.
- **Documents first.** The specification gives namespaces, module declarations and reference types before they are implemented, and the design the hooks, registries, declarations and names.

### 22. Beta

Make the language and its module API ready for external use: no breaking redesign is planned, the limitations that remain are documented, and a breaking change after it is exceptional, recorded and given migration guidance.

- **Needs:** nothing beyond what is built.
- **Done when:**
  - a cast inside an optional enclosed access is settled: which compositions the grammar allows and the parser refuses, as `(?((a ?? b) as "element").rect)` and `(?((?a.b) as "element").c)`, and why, are established, and the parser reads each one the grammar allows whose meaning is clear, the grammar staying as it is until that is known;
  - the module API is audited as the documents were, what `createModule`, `registerModule`, a declaration and the functions it names accept checked against what the design and the declarations say, each finding fixed or documented as a limitation. Known so far: what an observation's `sample` answers is not checked, so one answering a bare value reads as a sample that is never pending; `query.bindings` is public and typed, and whether it belongs to the API is decided; and a class instance a module answers for a structured type, as a `DOMRect` is, reads its fields through access and is copied as an empty object where the result holds it whole;
  - `CHANGELOG.md` exists, empty until the first change after the beta, from which it records each change a caller must act on, with how to migrate; nothing changed before the beta is recorded, since no caller depends on it;
  - the specification's version is `1.0.0-beta`, the package's `1.0.0-beta.N`, numbered by its commits as the alpha is, and a run from the sources reports `1.0.0-beta+source`, wherever a version is written or checked: the package project, the specification's and the design's titles, `Specification.mjs`, `domql.js`, the declarations, the agent guide's version rules, the CI workflow's comments, and the alignment and bundle tests.
- **State:** the specification, the design, the READMEs and the guides are audited and corrected, and a cast inside an optional enclosed access is settled, the parser reading both compositions the grammar allowed and refused; the module API is audited, registration refusing what it cannot honor, a structured answer taken by its declared fields, and a query's bindings no part of its API; the changelog and the version are not started.

## Departures from the design

A departure is recorded here as it happens, with the reason, until the design is brought into line.

- **The roots read the environment.** The design has Lexica pass the environment on without reading it, but until module namespaces give `domql.window` and `domql.document`, the evaluator reads the roots `window` and `document` from the environment's `window` and `document`.
- **Nodes are recognized by their `nodeType`.** Casts to node types, `==` between nodes, and the element each item of a projected list came from are decided in the evaluator by a value's `nodeType`, using no browser global, until reference types give DOMQL its own tests.
- **DOMQL reaches into Lexica's sources.** DOMQL imports the evaluator, the registry, resolution and the definition's helpers from `@formaui/lexica/language/…` until the host API gives it what it needs through the entry point.
