# Implementation decisions

The decisions taken while implementing the plan from step 5 of unit 1 through unit 5 without a review of each, so the work goes on uninterrupted: each is recorded here as it is taken, with its reason and what it departs from, if anything, and all of them are reviewed together before unit 6, when any is changed. A decision that settles what the specification leaves open is recorded in the specification too, where its subject lives, and links back here until its review.

**State:** awaiting review. An entry is reviewed once it is marked ✅ reviewed, or changed as its review decides.

| I7 | S1, the `indexer` kind | A used unnamed indexer, which declares no name, has the `name` null and the `qualifiedName` of its receiver type followed by brackets, `List[]`, as its access is written after its receiver; where its receiver type is any receiver or a type parameter, `[]` | The specification names neither; `qualifiedName` qualifies a member by its receiver type as `List.count` does, and brackets after the type say what the access writes | Nothing; a used declaration's `name` was always a string |

| I10 | S1, mappings | A mapping's key that is no string is refused with LEXICA4008, located at the declaration key `key`, and a mapping is located by its key | A key is any string, as a fixed argument gives it, so one that is no string is a mapping without its key, LEXICA4008's condition; the key tells a selector's mappings apart, as a name tells other declarations apart | The retired LEXICA4511, which refused a key by its spelling |
| I11 | S1, `internal` | A flat member's `target` counts among the types it writes: a public flat member targeting an internal type of its module is refused with LEXICA4052, and one targeting another module's internal type with LEXICA4018 | The specification refuses an extension's target naming another module's internal type, and bounds a member's accessibility by the type that holds it; until S2 gives a flat member an extension, its target is where its receiver is written, so it counts as the extension's target will | Nothing; S2 replaces the flat member's target with its extension's |
| I12 | S1, the scalar type names | `Type's` `PRIMITIVE_TYPE_NAMES`, the scalar names by `typeof`, stays `Type's` own, and a test asserts it names exactly `LexicaModule.scalarTypeNames` | `Type` is the leaf every stage builds on and imports nothing, and `LexicaModule` imports it; deriving the map from `LexicaModule` would make the leaf depend on the vocabulary, a cycle the test avoids while keeping the two lists one | The ledger's U7, which derives `PRIMITIVE_TYPE_NAMES` from the scalar list |
| I13 | S2 | S2 lands in four commits: extension types, with names that declare type parameters and scopes for them; the registration checks of type parameters; Lexica's own module, `List<T>` through its internal form and `VerbExtensions<T>`, with U6 and U7's built-in names; and the flat form removed, with M1's helpers, M6 and M15, every test migrated | Each commit builds on the one before and passes the gate alone; the flat form goes last, since every test module declares through it, and its migration then changes each test once | Nothing |

| I15 | S2, Lexica's own module | `Vocabulary` holds Lexica's value types through its internal form: `Number`, `String` and `Boolean` of kind `scalar`, and `List` of kind `generic`, its `typeParameters` names as every declaration's are, `['T']`, and its `variances` in their order, `['covariant']`, with the members of lists within it. Those members are validated, copied and frozen by a module created for that alone and never registered, an extension of `List<T>`; the registry registers them with Lexica's own module where it registers `Vocabulary.module`, by identity, and `VerbExtensions<T>` is an ordinary extension of that module. `getTypeDeclaration` replaces `getStructure`, `getReferenceType` and `getGenericType`, `isReferenceType` kept as the predicate it reads; `Vocabulary.builtInTypeNames` replaces `LexicaModule.builtInTypeNames`, derived from the value types | The specification gives Lexica's own module `List<T>` and no module form for a generic type, so the form stays Lexica's; a separate key of variances keeps `typeParameters` one shape, names, wherever it stands; module creation's own validation keeps one contract for every member; and the built-in names move to `Vocabulary`, which owns the types they name, since `LexicaModule` imports nothing of it | Nothing |
| I16 | S2, the duplicate checks | The duplicate tolerance check is module creation's LEXICA4026, which refused a tolerance on a scalar result of no number or a list before the registry's own check, which knows every type and refuses each of them, so registration alone checks it, creation keeping LEXICA4515's check of the number; the duplicate identifier check is `LexicaModule.#isIdentifier`, `Names.isIdentifier`'s pattern written again for an observation type's identity, which now reads `Names.isIdentifier`. | I17 | S2, the tests | A test that reached a receiver's member through its module's namespace, as a qualified form or a reference, now reads one the namespace still reaches: a member of a type the test's module owns, a word, a bag or a till of its own, or a module member of the value it was given, `vegan(word)`; a test of a receiver's member keeps it in an extension. | I18 | S2, the flat form's removal | `getReceiverTypes` keeps M15's name and answers a list, of the one type a member takes, its type or its extension's target, and of none for a module member, so no caller reads `'any'` or a list of targets; `target` on a member is refused as any key its kind does not take, LEXICA4030, with no hint of its own, as `predicates` loses its hint; the type checker reads a type parameter receiver as the least specific, which `'any'` was before it | M15's name was reviewed with a list in view, and one shape keeps every caller as it is; a migration hint describes the shape a host leaves, which the checkpoint's rows record instead | Nothing |
| I19 | Unit 1, step 6 | A call's value arguments are resolved for their evidence before its arguments are resolved in the order they are written, each against the scope of where it is written and nothing of it kept, through the speculative resolution a namespace's two readings use; an options object's value options stand in that scope by their names after their values, and its other options refuse a read, so a value option that reads a function option gives no evidence. | I20 | Unit 2, the selector | One `MemberSelector`, which the registry owns beside its `TypeChecker`, selects among a name's declarations, or the unnamed indexers, for a receiver type, answering the declaration, the competing ones, the internal ones that alone apply, or none: the resolver reads it for a member, a named and an unnamed indexer, a qualified call, the brackets after a name, and its hints. The owner of a type is the module that declares it, Lexica's own for Lexica's value types, and its members are those it declares within the types it owns, never its extensions. LEXICA3024 between modules names them and, where every competitor is an extension's, the extension accesses `receiver:Module.Extension.name` that select each; LEXICA3023 says the declaration is internal to its module, which no script reaches, for a member, an indexer, a namespace's member, a qualified form, a reference and a cast's or a conversion's type, a name that internal types alone have among them | Registration's refusal of competing declarations kept them unreachable where an extension access would select them; one selector keeps every path's precedence alike for the evaluation's deferred selection, which unit 2's third step adds; an extension access is written with its module so it selects one whatever the extensions' names | Nothing |
| I21 | Unit 2, operators | An operator is a member of kind `operator`, within a type or an extension, named `cast` or `convert`, its destination its `result`, its own declaration keys `name`, `kind`, `result`, `impl` and `internal`; the registry indexes operators by name apart from the members, and a namespace reaches none. `to` and `as` select theirs among the operators of that name and destination applicable to the source, through the selector. A message names one by its receiver, its syntax and its destination, `String to Number` and `Meter as Home.Device`, and an error locates it at its module, the type or the extension it stands in, and its name. Lexica's own conversions are operators of its scalar types, declared through its internal form as the members of lists are. A declared cast's `impl` and a conversion's are called alike, a rejection answering the reason; a cast's other failures, a reason that is no string among them, are LEXICA5004, and a conversion's keep LEXICA5003 and LEXICA5018. LEXICA4067 is raised by nothing, since a conversion within a type converts from that type and one in an extension from its target | The specification's operators are members its syntax alone invokes, so their declaration, accessibility and selection are a member's, and an operator's name says which syntax invokes it; two modules can't compete for one conversion, since each would need the other registered before it, so its selection is the owner's or the one applicable extension's | Nothing |
